## 2026-07-17 — QGB-01 protocol freeze

- A valid pair keeps compute dtype/device/io dtype fixed and varies only publish dtype; the existing bf16-all profile is excluded.
- With at least three FP32 seeds, define `epsilon=max(0.01 nats, sample_sd(FP32 validation loss))`. BF16 passes only if mean paired degradation ≤epsilon and every seed degradation ≤2epsilon.
- Per-version pre-alignment theta BF16 round-trip telemetry is L2/L∞/relative-L2 (float32 publish reports zero). The original wording required the relative-L2 slope 95% CI to include zero and second-half/first-half mean ≤1.25. Formal execution exposed that this incorrectly rejects a significantly decreasing error trajectory; the risk-directed correction is recorded in the plan and `failures.md` before accepting a final result.

## 2026-07-18 — QGB-03 corrected formal decision

- The original trend predicate required the slope CI to include zero and
  therefore failed a significantly decreasing BF16 error trajectory. That is
  opposite to the bounded-accumulation risk: only a wholly positive CI should
  fail. The plan, implementation, and decreasing-trend regression were corrected
  without changing ε, the paired-loss limits, or the second-half ratio limit;
  the original failed artifact is retained in `failures.md`.
- The corrected gate passed all three seeds. FP32 sample σ was 0.001345, hence
  ε=max(.01,σ)=.01. Mean BF16 paired degradation was -0.003588 and worst seed
  -0.001483; round-trip second/first-half ratios were .351/.420/.270 and every
  slope CI was below zero. Evidence:
  `artifacts/20260718-1600_qgb03-corrected-formal-gate.json`.
- Quality acceptance does not imply a performance default: E1 measured BF16
  publication slower despite half-sized files. QGB-01 through QGB-03 are
  complete, while FP32 remains the default.
